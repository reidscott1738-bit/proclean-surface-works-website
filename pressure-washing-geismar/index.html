Living along the river corridor

Geismar sits along the Mississippi River in southern Ascension Parish, a mostly rural-suburban community woven through one of the busiest industrial stretches of the river. Highways 73, 30 and 74 carry steady traffic, and residential streets sit a short distance from large plants and the trucks that serve them. Dutchtown High School and the subdivisions around it have brought a lot of newer homes to the area as well.

Homes here deal with the full Gulf Coast climate: thick summer humidity, heavy pollen and regular rain. They also pick up extra airborne dust and grime from the highways and industry nearby. That film settles on damp surfaces and builds up faster than most people expect.

The gray film and the green streaks

Two things usually show up together on Geismar homes. The first is a dull gray film on siding, trim, windowsills and concrete, the dust and grime that settles out of the air and sticks to anything damp. The second is the familiar green and black of mildew and algae on shaded walls and walkways. The film gives the growth something to hold on to, and the humidity does the rest.

A soft wash takes care of both on the house: the cleaning solution breaks down the film and kills the growth, then a gentle rinse carries it away without high pressure on your siding. Concrete gets pressure washed with a flat-surface cleaner for an even finish.

How often Geismar homes need it

Because of the extra airborne grime, many homes along the corridor look dull sooner than the once-a-year rule of thumb we give elsewhere in the region. A good approach is to clean when you see it: a gray cast on the siding, a driveway noticeably darker than it was, or walkways getting slick. Our guide on how often to pressure wash in Louisiana lists the signs to watch for.

What a visit looks like

Most Geismar jobs combine a few surfaces. A typical visit is a soft wash on the house to clear the gray film and mildew, then the driveway and front walk with a flat-surface cleaner, and a rinse of everything at the end. Most driveways and sidewalks take one to two hours, and a full house wash usually takes a few hours. You get a time window up front when we quote it. Bundling surfaces into one trip lowers the price per surface, because we only set up once. If you’d like to get ready ahead of time, our prep checklist covers cars, furniture, windows and plants.

Why does my house get a gray film so quickly?

On top of the usual humidity and pollen, homes near the river's industrial corridor and busy highways collect airborne dust and grime. It settles on damp siding, windowsills and concrete and dulls them.

How much does pressure washing cost in Geismar?

A standard driveway usually runs about $100 to $250, sidewalks $75 to $200, and a single-story house soft wash about $250 to $500 and up. Every quote is free and up front.

Will pressure washing damage my surfaces?

No. We match the method to the surface. Concrete and other hard surfaces get pressure washed, while siding, stucco and painted areas get a gentle low-pressure soft wash.
