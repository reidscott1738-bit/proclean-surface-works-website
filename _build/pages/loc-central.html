River country inside the parish

The City of Central, incorporated in 2005, is a tight-knit residential community in the northeast corner of East Baton Rouge Parish, threaded by the Comite and Amite rivers. That river setting is part of daily life here. Homeowners who lived through the August 2016 flood know how quickly water, and the silt and grime it leaves behind, can change a property.

Even in normal years, the humidity that hangs over the river bottoms feeds mildew on concrete and siding. Low, wooded lots dry slowly after rain, and the shaded sides of houses near the trees grow algae faster than homes in more open parts of the parish.

Big lots, long driveways, lots of brick

Central is mostly single-family homes on larger lots along Hooper Road, Sullivan Road, Wax Road and Greenwell Springs Road. Many have long concrete driveways and plenty of exposed brick and siding. Those big surfaces show mildew streaks and dark staining clearly once it sets in, and they’re exactly what a proper cleaning transforms.

Long driveways are where technique shows. Cleaning a long run of concrete with just a wand leaves stripes and uneven patches. We use a flat-surface cleaner, which keeps the spray at a steady height and overlaps each pass, so the whole driveway comes out one even color. On brick and siding we switch to a low-pressure soft wash so mortar and paint aren’t damaged.

Protecting what you’ve built

Mildew and algae aren’t just cosmetic. They hold moisture against brick, siding and paint and shorten their life, and once staining sets in deep enough it becomes permanent. A yearly cleaning keeps buildup from ever reaching that point, so each visit is quick and affordable instead of a heavy restoration job.
