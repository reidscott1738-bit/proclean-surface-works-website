What’s really growing on your siding

The dull green film creeping across vinyl, the dark streaks running down from the roofline, the gray cast on the north wall: none of that is just dirt. It is mildew and algae, and South Louisiana is close to an ideal place for it to grow. Warm, damp air hangs around most of the year, rain keeps walls wet, and anything under tree cover or facing north dries slowly and grows the green fastest.

Left alone, that growth holds moisture against your exterior and shortens the life of paint, caulk and siding. It also makes a well-kept home look tired from the street.

Why we soft wash instead of blasting

High-pressure water is the wrong tool for the house itself. It can force water behind siding panels and into the wall, chip or etch stucco, strip paint and gouge wood. Most of the “pressure washing ruined my house” stories are just the wrong method on the wrong surface.

Soft washing barely uses pressure at all, roughly the force of a garden hose. Instead of force, it relies on a cleaning solution with a mildewcide that kills mold, mildew, algae and bacteria. We let it dwell, then rinse everything away gently. Because the growth is killed rather than knocked off the surface, a soft-washed house stays clean noticeably longer than one that was simply sprayed down. How often to wash a house here

For most Louisiana homes, about once a year is right. Shaded walls, homes under big oaks or pines, and light-colored siding show growth sooner and may need it more often. Late spring, after the heavy pollen falls, and fall, before the holidays, are both good times to schedule. Our guide on how often to pressure wash your house has the full schedule and the signs that it’s time.

Roof streaks? Dark streaks that start on the roof wash down onto the siding. A roof should never be pressure washed; ask us about it when you request your quote.
