A growing town east of Denham Springs

Walker is a fast-growing Livingston Parish community just east of Denham Springs along Interstate 12, home to many families who work across the capital region. New subdivisions off Highway 447 and Walker South Road have added a lot of brick homes with fresh concrete driveways, while older parts of town have more mature trees.

The whole area sits under the same heavy humidity and long growing season that coats surfaces in mildew and algae. Like much of Livingston Parish, Walker felt the August 2016 flood, and residents here take property upkeep seriously.

New concrete still darkens

A common surprise for Walker homeowners is how quickly a newer driveway changes color. Concrete is porous from the day it’s poured. Sprinklers and lawn runoff keep the edges wet, wooden privacy fences shade the side yard, and within a few years the borders, the apron and the garage approach are noticeably darker than the middle. The garage apron in the photo above is a typical example.

The good news is that early staining comes out easily. The longer it goes, the deeper it sets, so cleaning newer concrete before it gets bad is the cheapest way to keep it looking new.

Bigger lots, longer driveways

Outside the newest subdivisions, many properties in and around Walker sit on larger lots with longer driveways. Long runs of concrete are where a flat-surface cleaner earns its keep: it keeps the spray at a steady height and overlaps each pass, so a long driveway comes out one even color instead of streaked. Long drives are priced by size, and bundling the drive, walkways and house in one visit keeps the cost per surface down.
