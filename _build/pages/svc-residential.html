Your home against the Louisiana climate

For most families a house is the biggest thing they’ll ever own, and the weather here works against it every day. Heat and humidity grow mildew on concrete and siding. Spring coats everything in yellow pollen. Frequent rain keeps shaded surfaces damp long enough for algae to settle in, and storm season leaves its own layer of grime and debris.

None of it happens overnight, which is why it’s easy to miss. Then one day a neighbor gets their driveway cleaned and yours suddenly looks two shades darker. Regular cleaning keeps that buildup from turning into permanent staining and keeps your home looking cared for.

Right pressure, right surface

Most damage blamed on pressure washing comes from using the wrong method. Concrete can take real pressure, and a flat-surface cleaner gets it evenly clean without stripes. Siding, stucco, paint and wood can’t take that force. They get a soft wash: a cleaning solution that kills mildew and algae, then a gentle rinse. On a typical house we use both in the same appointment. If you want the details, read soft washing vs pressure washing.
