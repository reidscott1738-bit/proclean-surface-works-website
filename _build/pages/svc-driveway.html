Why Baton Rouge driveways turn dark

Concrete looks solid, but it is porous, more like a hard sponge than a sealed slab. It soaks up everything that lands on it: oil drips, tire rubber, pollen, leaf tannins and the general grime of a humid climate. In South Louisiana the bigger problem is living growth. Mildew and algae settle into those pores, feed on the moisture that hangs in the air most of the year, and slowly turn a bright gray driveway dark and blotchy.

It happens so gradually that most homeowners forget what their concrete is supposed to look like. Shaded strips under oaks, the edges along the lawn and the apron by the street usually go first. A garden hose won’t touch it, because the growth is down in the surface, not sitting on top.

How we clean a driveway

We use commercial pressure and a flat-surface cleaner, a round hood with spinning spray arms underneath. It cleans the slab in overlapping passes at a consistent distance, which is what gives you an even color with no zebra stripes. A wand on its own can’t do that on a large flat area.

  1. Look the job over. From your photos or an on-site look, we note staining, nearby plants and anything you want extra attention on.
  2. Pretreat problem spots. Oil and rust need their own treatment before the main clean, or they just lighten instead of lifting.
  3. Surface-clean the whole slab. Driveway, apron and connected walkways get the same even pass so you don’t end up with one bright patch on a dingy slab.
  4. Detail the edges. Expansion joints, curbs and the borders along the grass get cleaned with a wand.
  5. Rinse everything down. Debris gets washed off the concrete, the lawn edge and the street so the job looks finished.

An honest word on oil stains

Fresh and moderate oil stains usually come up well with a degreaser pretreatment and hot water. Very old stains that have been baking into the concrete for years sometimes only fade. We’ll tell you which you’re dealing with before we start, not after. If you want to try a spot yourself first, our step-by-step guide on removing oil stains from a driveway covers what actually works.

Keeping it clean afterward

In our climate, once or twice a year keeps a driveway from building up permanent mildew staining. Shaded driveways need the higher end. Sealing the concrete after a cleaning fills the pores so the next spill sits on top where you can wipe it up.
