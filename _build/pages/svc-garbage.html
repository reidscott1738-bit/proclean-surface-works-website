Why trash cans get so bad here

Every bag that goes in a can leaves something behind: a drip of juice, a split bag, food scraps that miss the bag entirely. In a mild climate that residue just dries out. In Baton Rouge, where summer days are hot and humid and a dark plastic cart can sit in full sun all afternoon, it does the opposite. The bottom of the can becomes warm, damp and full of bacteria breaking down whatever is in there.

That’s where the smell comes from, and it’s why cans draw flies and, if they go long enough, maggots. Rinsing with a garden hose moves the mess around but rarely gets the film off the walls and the bottom corners where it builds up.

Keeping a can fresh between cleanings

A few habits help a clean can stay that way longer:

  • Tie bags tightly and double-bag anything wet, like seafood shells or meat scraps.
  • Keep the can in shade if you can. A can out of the afternoon sun stays cooler and smells less.
  • Leave the lid open for an hour after pickup on a dry day so the inside can air out.
  • Sprinkle a little baking soda in the bottom to absorb odor and moisture.
