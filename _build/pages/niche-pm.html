Three problems, one crew

Managing rental property means juggling turnovers, curb appeal and liability, often across several addresses at once. Exterior cleaning touches all three.

Turnovers

Between tenants, a quick exterior clean makes a unit show better, rent faster and hold its price. Prospective tenants judge a property from the parking lot and the walk to the door, and a dark driveway or a mildewed breezeway tells them how the place is maintained.

Liability

Dirty walkways aren’t just unsightly. In the Baton Rouge climate, shaded concrete grows a slick film of algae, and a wet, algae-covered path or stair landing is a genuine slip hazard you’re responsible for. Keeping common walkways clean is one of the simplest risk reductions available.

Owner expectations

Owners notice when a property looks neglected. A regular cleaning schedule keeps exteriors in line with what owners expect without you having to chase it.

  2. Get per-property pricing. A clear number for each address so you can bill owners accurately.
  3. Schedule turnovers or a recurring plan. One-off make-readies, or a standing schedule for common areas.

Recurring service for common areas

For duplexes, fourplexes and small complexes, the common walkways, stairs and breezeways get dirty on a predictable cycle. Putting them on a quarterly or twice-a-year schedule keeps them consistently clean and safe and makes the cost easy to budget. Recurring visits are usually quicker than letting buildup get heavy.

Single-family rentals

For single-family rentals, the most common request is a turnover package: driveway, front walk, porch and a soft wash on the front of the house. It’s the same set of surfaces that matters for a home going on the market, because the goal is the same: make a good first impression on the next person through the door.
