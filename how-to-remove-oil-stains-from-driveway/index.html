How to remove oil stains from a driveway

Why oil stains are stubborn

Concrete is porous, more like a hard sponge than a solid slab, so motor oil soaks down into it instead of sitting on top. That's why a quick rinse or even a normal pressure wash often just lightens an oil stain instead of removing it. The oil that's soaked below the surface has to be pulled back out, not just blasted off the top.

The DIY approach that actually helps

Act fast on fresh spills. Cover a fresh spill with cat litter or baking soda to absorb what hasn't soaked in yet, leave it a few hours, then sweep it up.

Degrease and scrub. Apply a concrete degreaser or a strong grease-cutting dish soap, let it dwell, then scrub with a stiff brush and rinse with the hottest water you can. Repeat for stubborn spots.

Use a poultice for old stains. For set-in stains, a poultice, a degreaser mixed into an absorbent powder into a paste, spread on, covered, and left to dry, can draw oil back out of the pores over a day or two.

When to call a pro

Old, deep, or large oil stains usually need commercial-grade degreasers, hot water, and proper pressure equipment to get real results, and even then, very old stains may only improve rather than vanish completely. A good pro will tell you honestly what to expect before starting.

Pro Clean Surface Works pretreats oil and rust staining and cleans the whole driveway to an even finish, so you don't end up with one clean patch on a dingy slab. If a stain won't fully come out, we'll say so up front.

Preventing the next one

Once your driveway is clean, a concrete sealer helps by filling the pores so future spills sit on top where they're easy to wipe up. Keeping a bag of cat litter in the garage for fast spills is the simplest prevention there is.

Questions

Straight answers.

Can you completely remove old oil stains?+

Often we can remove or greatly reduce them with commercial degreasers and hot pressure washing. Very old, deep stains sometimes only improve, and we'll tell you honestly what to expect before we start.

Will pressure washing alone remove oil?+

Usually not on its own. Oil soaks into porous concrete, so it needs a degreaser pretreatment plus hot water and pressure to pull it back out, not just a surface rinse.

We clean the whole driveway to an even finish so you're not left with one clean spot on a dirty slab.
