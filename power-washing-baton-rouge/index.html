Power washing vs pressure washing vs soft washing

Strictly speaking, power washing uses heated water and pressure washing uses unheated water at high pressure. Soft washing uses very little pressure and relies on a cleaning solution instead. In everyday use the first two words mean the same thing, and that’s fine. The distinction that actually matters for your property is high pressure versus low pressure, and which surfaces can take which.

Which surfaces get which method

High pressure

Concrete driveways, sidewalks, curbs, patios, pool decks and most brick or paver hardscape. These are tough enough to take real force. On big flat areas we use a flat-surface cleaner, which keeps the spray at a steady distance and leaves one even color instead of stripes.

Low pressure (soft wash)

Vinyl and painted siding, stucco, soffits, eaves, screens, wood and roofs. High pressure can force water behind siding, chip stucco, strip paint, gouge wood and tear granules off shingles. These surfaces get a cleaning solution that kills mildew and algae, then a gentle rinse.

In between

Wood fences and decks sit in the middle. They can be cleaned, but with less pressure and more care than concrete so the grain isn’t torn up. That takes longer.

Why the method matters more than the machine

Anyone can rent a pressure washer for the weekend. The difference with a professional is knowing, without guessing, which surface gets which treatment, and having the right attachments to get an even result. Most of the “pressure washing ruined my house” stories come down to the wrong pressure on the wrong surface: a wand held too close to vinyl, or stripes left across a driveway. Power washing questions

Is power washing the same as pressure washing?

In everyday use, yes, and most people use the terms interchangeably. Technically power washing uses heated water, but what matters is matching the method and pressure to the surface, which is what we focus on.

What is soft washing?

Soft washing uses low pressure plus a cleaning solution that kills mold, mildew and algae. It is the safe method for siding, stucco, painted surfaces, wood and roofs.

Can you power wash a fence or deck?

Yes. Wood and vinyl fences and decks can be cleaned, but wood needs a gentler touch than concrete so the grain is not gouged.

Will power washing damage my surfaces?

No. We match the method to the surface. Concrete and other hard surfaces get pressure washed, while siding, stucco and painted areas get a gentle low-pressure soft wash so nothing is harmed.
