Denham Springs and its surfaces

Denham Springs anchors western Livingston Parish, just across the Amite River from Baton Rouge along Interstate 12. It’s known for its Antique Village downtown and a wide spread of family neighborhoods around it, plus the busy shopping around the Juban Road exit. Like the rest of the region it lives under heavy humidity, frequent rain and a long growing season for the mildew and algae that dull concrete and siding.

Many homes here are brick with concrete drives and walkways. Brick hides dirt better than vinyl for a while, but once mildew and algae settle into the texture and the mortar joints, the whole house takes on a gray-green cast. Concrete next to it darkens at the same time, and the two together can make a well-kept home look older than it is.

After the 2016 flood

Denham Springs was one of the hardest-hit communities in the August 2016 flood, and a lot of homes and businesses here were rebuilt or renovated afterward. Residents learned the hard way how quickly water, silt and grime can change a property. Even in normal years, the low ground near the Amite and its tributaries stays damp, and the shaded sides of houses near the water grow algae faster.

Keeping concrete and siding clean isn’t a flood fix, but it does protect the work that went into rebuilding. Mildew and algae hold moisture against brick, siding and paint, and regular cleaning keeps that from turning into permanent staining.

Brick needs a gentle hand

It’s tempting to hit brick with high pressure because it looks tough. The brick itself usually is; the mortar between it isn’t. Pressure aimed at mortar joints can erode them over time and drive water into the wall. We soft wash brick instead: a cleaning solution that kills the mildew and algae, time to work, and a gentle rinse. The result is cleaner and it lasts longer, because the growth is killed rather than just knocked back. More on that in soft washing vs pressure washing.
