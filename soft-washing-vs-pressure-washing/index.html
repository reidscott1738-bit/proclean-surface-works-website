Soft washing vs pressure washing: which does your home need?

Which surfaces need high pressure, which need a gentle soft wash, and why using the wrong one is how surfaces get damaged.

The difference in one sentence

Pressure washing blasts away grime with high-pressure water and is right for hard surfaces like concrete; soft washing uses low pressure plus a cleaning solution to safely kill and remove mold and algae from delicate surfaces like siding and roofs. Using the wrong one is exactly how surfaces get damaged, so matching the method to the surface is the whole job.

How pressure washing works

Pressure washing relies on force. A machine pushes water through a nozzle at high pressure, often through a flat-surface cleaner that spins two jets under a round hood, to physically scour dirt, mildew and stains off durable surfaces. On concrete, that flat-surface cleaner is what leaves an even, stripe-free finish instead of the zebra striping you get from waving a wand around.

It’s the right tool for driveways, sidewalks, curbs, patios, pool decks and most hardscape, surfaces tough enough to take the force without harm. You can see the kind of result it gets on our before and after page; every one of those jobs is concrete.

How soft washing works

Soft washing barely uses pressure at all, about the force of a garden hose. Instead of blasting the surface, it applies a cleaning solution containing a mildewcide that kills mold, mildew, algae and bacteria at the root, lets it dwell, and then rinses everything away gently.

Because it kills the growth instead of just knocking off the top layer, a soft wash actually stays clean noticeably longer than a high-pressure rinse of the same surface. It’s the standard, safe method for anything that can’t take force.

On a typical house we use both on the same visit: high pressure on the driveway and walkways, a gentle soft wash on the siding and eaves.

What happens when the wrong method is used

High pressure on siding can force water behind the panels and into the wall, crack or chip the surface, and strip paint. On stucco it can etch and pit the finish. On brick walls it can erode mortar joints. On a roof it can tear granules off asphalt shingles and void some manufacturer warranties. On wood it gouges and splinters the grain.

Most of the “pressure washing ruined my house” horror stories are really just the wrong method on the wrong surface. That’s the real value of hiring someone who does this regularly: knowing, without guessing, which surface gets which treatment.

Why it matters more in Louisiana

In drier climates, a lot of exterior grime is just dust and dirt, and a rinse takes care of it. In South Louisiana most of what darkens a house is living growth: mildew and algae feeding on constant humidity. Knocking the top layer off with pressure leaves the roots behind, and it grows back fast. Killing it with a soft wash solution is what actually keeps a house clean through a humid summer. For how often that needs to happen, see how often to wash your house in Louisiana.

How to tell which your home needs

A simple rule: if it’s flat and made of concrete or pavers, it can usually be pressure washed. If it’s part of the house itself, siding, stucco, brick walls, wood or the roof, it should be soft washed. Green or black streaks on siding and dark streaks on a roof are classic signs of living algae that a soft wash is designed to kill.

What about “power washing”?

Technically, power washing means pressure washing with heated water. In everyday use people say power washing, pressure washing and even soft washing interchangeably. The term you search doesn’t matter much; the method used on each surface does. Our power washing page covers that.

Soft wash questions

Still unsure? Call or text (225) 363-5220.

Is soft washing safe for my siding?

Yes. Soft washing is specifically the safe method for siding, stucco and painted surfaces because it uses low pressure and a cleaning solution instead of force. It won't damage the surface the way high pressure can.

Which method does my roof need?

A roof should always be soft washed, never pressure washed. High pressure damages shingles and can void warranties. A low-pressure treatment kills the black algae streaks safely.

Does soft washing stay clean longer?

Yes. Because it kills the mold and algae at the root instead of just rinsing the surface, a soft wash typically stays clean noticeably longer than a high-pressure rinse.

Can you do both on one visit?

Yes, and we usually do, pressure washing the concrete and soft washing the house exterior in the same appointment.

Is brick safe to pressure wash?

Brick pavers and brick hardscape can usually take pressure. Brick walls on a house are better soft washed, because high pressure aimed at mortar joints can erode them and drive water into the wall.
