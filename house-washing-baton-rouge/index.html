What’s really growing on your siding

The dull green film creeping across vinyl, the dark streaks running down from the roofline, the gray cast on the north wall: none of that is just dirt. It is mildew and algae, and South Louisiana is close to an ideal place for it to grow. Warm, damp air hangs around most of the year, rain keeps walls wet, and anything under tree cover or facing north dries slowly and grows the green fastest.

Left alone, that growth holds moisture against your exterior and shortens the life of paint, caulk and siding. It also makes a well-kept home look tired from the street.

Why we soft wash instead of blasting

High-pressure water is the wrong tool for the house itself. It can force water behind siding panels and into the wall, chip or etch stucco, strip paint and gouge wood. Most of the “pressure washing ruined my house” stories are just the wrong method on the wrong surface.

Soft washing barely uses pressure at all, roughly the force of a garden hose. Instead of force, it relies on a cleaning solution with a mildewcide that kills mold, mildew, algae and bacteria. We let it dwell, then rinse everything away gently. Because the growth is killed rather than knocked off the surface, a soft-washed house stays clean noticeably longer than one that was simply sprayed down. We cover the method in detail in soft washing vs pressure washing.

How a house wash goes

  1. Send a couple of photos. A shot of each side of the house lets us size it up and price it.
  2. Get one clear price. House washing is priced by wall area and the number of stories, since height slows the work.
  3. We soft wash it. Solution on, time to work, then a gentle rinse from top to bottom.
  4. Concrete, if you want it. Most customers add the driveway and walkways, which get pressure washed on the same visit.

House washing cost

A single-story home usually runs about $250 to $500 and up, with two-story and larger homes costing more. Heavy growth, tall walls and tight access add time. Bundling the house with your driveway and walkways is almost always cheaper per surface than separate visits. See the full breakdown in our cost guide.

How often to wash a house here

For most Louisiana homes, about once a year is right. Shaded walls, homes under big oaks or pines, and light-colored siding show growth sooner and may need it more often. Late spring, after the heavy pollen falls, and fall, before the holidays, are both good times to schedule. Our guide on how often to pressure wash your house has the full schedule and the signs that it’s time.

Roof streaks? Dark streaks that start on the roof wash down onto the siding. A roof should never be pressure washed; ask us about it when you request your quote.

What is the difference between soft washing and pressure washing?

Pressure washing uses high-pressure water and is right for hard surfaces like concrete. Soft washing uses low pressure plus a cleaning solution and is the safe choice for siding, stucco and painted surfaces. We use whichever the surface calls for.

How much does a house wash cost?

A single-story soft wash typically runs about $250 to $500, with two-story and larger homes costing more. Price tracks the wall area and the number of stories. Send a photo of the house for an exact price.

How often should a house be washed in Louisiana?

About once a year for most homes here. Shaded, north-facing or light-colored walls may need it sooner because they show mildew and algae streaks first.

Will soft washing damage my siding or paint?

No. Soft washing is the safe method for siding, stucco and painted surfaces because it relies on a cleaning solution and gentle rinsing instead of force.

Does a soft wash stay clean longer?

Yes. Because it kills mold and algae at the root instead of just rinsing the surface, a soft wash typically stays clean noticeably longer than a high-pressure rinse.

Can you clean the driveway on the same visit?

Yes, and we usually do. The house gets a soft wash and the concrete gets pressure washed in the same appointment, which lowers the price per surface.
