New neighborhoods, same Louisiana problem

Prairieville sits between Baton Rouge and Gonzales in Ascension Parish, and over the last couple of decades it has turned from open land into one of the busiest residential areas in the region. Subdivision after subdivision has gone in along Highway 42, Highway 73 and Highway 44, and many families moved here for newer homes and the Ascension Parish schools.

Newer doesn’t mean immune. A five-year-old driveway can already have dark edges along the grass and a green tint where the sprinklers hit. Light-colored siding, which is common on newer builds, shows mildew streaks sooner than darker colors. And many lots back up to tree lines and drainage canals that keep the back of the house shaded and damp.

What we see most in Prairieville

  • Dark driveway edges and aprons. Water from lawns and sprinklers runs onto the concrete and feeds mildew along the borders first.
  • Green on the back of the house. Homes that back up to woods or drainage stay shaded and grow algae on siding and soffits.
  • Covered patios going dark. Back patios under the roofline barely see sun and mildew quickly.
  • Pollen film in spring. Everything turns yellow-green for a few weeks, and it sticks to damp surfaces.

Curb appeal matters here

In neighborhoods where homes turn over regularly and many streets have HOA standards, curb appeal is more than vanity. A clean driveway, bright walkway and fresh siding help a house stand out when it’s listed and keep it in line with neighborhood expectations the rest of the time. If you’re selling, our page for realtors and home sellers explains how we schedule around listing photos. If you sit on an HOA board, see pressure washing for HOAs.

How we clean

Concrete driveways, sidewalks and patios get commercial pressure and a flat-surface cleaner for an even finish with no stripes. Siding, brick and stucco get a low-pressure soft wash that kills the mildew instead of blasting the surface. Most Prairieville customers bundle the driveway, walkways and house in one visit, which lowers the price per surface.

Prairieville pressure washing questions

Still unsure? Call or text (225) 363-5220.

Do you serve all of Prairieville?

Yes, all of Prairieville and the surrounding Ascension Parish neighborhoods, including the subdivisions off Highway 42, Highway 73, Highway 44 and Airline Highway.

My house is only a few years old. Does it really need cleaning?

Often, yes. Newer concrete and siding face the same heat, humidity and pollen as older homes, and shaded lots in newer subdivisions hold moisture. Dark edges on a five-year-old driveway are common.

How much does pressure washing cost in Prairieville?

The same as in Baton Rouge. A standard driveway usually runs about $100 to $250 and a single-story house soft wash about $250 to $500 and up. Quotes are free and up front.

Can you clean before my HOA inspection or a home sale?

Yes. Tell us your deadline and we will schedule around it. Many Prairieville subdivisions have HOA standards, and a clean driveway and exterior help with both inspections and listing photos.

Will pressure washing damage my siding?

No. Siding, stucco and painted surfaces get a low-pressure soft wash. High pressure is only used on concrete and other hard surfaces.
