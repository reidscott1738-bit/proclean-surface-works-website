The entrance sets the tone

A neighborhood’s entrance is the first thing residents and prospective buyers see. A mildew-streaked monument sign, a green and slippery common sidewalk or a stained pool deck quietly drags down the whole community’s impression. For an HOA board, keeping shared surfaces clean is a curb-appeal decision that protects home values and a safety decision that reduces slip-and-fall risk on common walkways.

Around Baton Rouge, Prairieville, Gonzales and the surrounding parishes, plenty of subdivisions have brick or stucco entrance monuments, long common sidewalks and amenity centers with pool decks. All of them face the same humidity and shade that turn concrete dark and walls green.

Built for how boards make decisions

Boards need numbers they can put in front of members and a budget they can plan around. We keep it simple:

  1. Tell us what’s shared. Send photos or a list of the entrance, sidewalks, amenity areas and anything else the association maintains.
  2. Get an itemized quote. One clear price, broken out by area if that helps the board decide what to approve.
  3. Pick one-time or recurring. A refresh before a busy season or annual meeting, or a standing quarterly or annual schedule.
  4. We schedule around residents. Early in the day, off-days, and around pool hours and neighborhood events.

Gentle where it counts

Entrance monuments are often brick, stone, stucco or painted, sometimes with lettering or lighting. High pressure can etch stucco, erode mortar and lift paint. We soft wash those surfaces with a low-pressure cleaning solution that kills mildew and algae, and save high pressure for the concrete. More on the difference in soft washing vs pressure washing.

Recurring service

Common areas get dirty on a predictable cycle, especially shaded sidewalks and pool decks. A quarterly or annual schedule keeps them consistently clean and makes the cost predictable for the budget. Recurring visits are usually quicker and cheaper per visit than letting buildup get heavy.

Residents, too

Some associations like to let residents know when we’ll be in the neighborhood so homeowners can book their own driveway or house wash at the same time. If your board wants to do that, just mention it when you reach out.

Questions

Questions from HOA boards

Still unsure? Call or text (225) 363-5220.

Do you offer recurring cleaning for HOAs?

Yes. Many associations set up quarterly or annual cleanings for entrances, sidewalks and amenity areas so everything stays consistent. We will recommend a schedule and give you one clear price to take to the board.

Can you give the board an itemized quote?

Yes. We provide a clear, up-front quote you can present for approval, and we can break it out by area, such as entrance, sidewalks and amenity center, if that helps.

Can you clean around residents and pool hours?

Yes. We can schedule early in the day or on off-days and work around pool hours and events so residents are not in the way.

Will pressure washing damage the entrance sign?

No. Monument signs, painted surfaces and stucco get a low-pressure soft wash. High pressure is only used on concrete and other hard surfaces.

Do you offer free quotes?

Yes, always. Text a couple of photos of what you need cleaned and we can usually give you a price the same day, with no obligation to book.
