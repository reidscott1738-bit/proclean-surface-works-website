What Baton Rouge weather does to your concrete and siding

Baton Rouge sits on the Mississippi River in one of the hottest, most humid corners of the Gulf South, and it gets a lot of rain, close to five feet in a typical year. That combination is hard on every outdoor surface. Long, muggy summers grow a film of green and black mildew on concrete and north-facing walls. Spring coats everything in a heavy layer of yellow pollen. Afternoon storms keep driveways damp long enough for algae to take hold, and hurricane season leaves its own grime and storm residue behind.

The result looks the same all over town. A driveway that was bright gray a few years ago slowly turns dark and streaky. Siding picks up a dingy shadow along the shaded side. Walkways under the eaves get slick. Most people don’t notice how far it has drifted until they see it cleaned.

Old trees, older neighborhoods, faster growth

A lot of Baton Rouge’s best streets are also its shadiest. The live oaks lining the Garden District, Southdowns and the neighborhoods around the LSU lakes are a big part of why people love living there, but that shade keeps concrete and brick damp and gives mildew a head start. Older driveways and walkways in Mid City and Spanish Town have decades of staining baked in. Newer subdivisions off Highland Road and Perkins Road have fresh concrete, and even that starts darkening along the edges within a few years.

We see the same handful of problems over and over:

  • Driveways gone dark and blotchy, worst under trees and along the lawn edge.
  • Green and black streaks on vinyl siding and the shaded side of brick homes.
  • Slick walkways and front entries that never get direct sun.
  • Covered back patios turned almost black with mildew.
  • Curbs and street-side sidewalks stained by runoff and tire grime.

Why does my Baton Rouge driveway get dark so fast?

Heat, humidity and frequent rain keep concrete damp long enough for mildew and algae to grow in the surface. Shade from live oaks and other mature trees, common in older Baton Rouge neighborhoods, makes it worse.
