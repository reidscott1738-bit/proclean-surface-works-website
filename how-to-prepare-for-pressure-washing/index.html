How to prepare for pressure washing: a homeowner checklist

A few minutes of prep the day before makes the cleaning faster, more even and safer for your plants, pets and belongings. Here’s what to do.

The quick checklist

  1. Move cars off the driveway and away from the house.
  2. Clear furniture, planters, grills, toys and doormats from the areas being cleaned.
  3. Close and latch windows and doors on the sides being washed.
  4. Keep pets inside or away from the work area.
  5. Unlock gates and make sure an outdoor spigot is working.
  6. Tell us about delicate plants, loose trim, cracked concrete or anything else to watch for.

That’s most of it. The sections below explain why each step matters.

Cars and the driveway

A car parked on the driveway leaves a rectangle that can’t be cleaned, and when it’s moved later, that patch stands out against the clean concrete around it. Move vehicles to the street or a neighbor’s drive before we start, and keep them away from the house if siding is being washed, so they don’t get splashed with cleaning solution or rinse water.

If there’s an oil leak under the usual parking spot, mention it. Oil gets pretreated before the main clean, and we’ll tell you up front if an old stain is likely to fade rather than fully disappear.

Patio furniture and outdoor items

For patios and porches, clear everything you can move: chairs, tables, planters, grill covers, doormats and kids’ toys. An empty slab cleans evenly. Items left in place leave outlines, and rust or dirt rings under furniture feet are often the spots you most want cleaned. If something is too heavy to move, tell us when you book and we’ll plan around it.

Windows, doors and vents

Close and latch every window and door on the sides of the house being washed. Even a soft wash involves a lot of water near the walls, and a window that looks closed but isn’t latched can let water in. If you have a dryer vent or other opening near ground level, let us know.

Plants and landscaping

The cleaning solution used in a soft wash is meant for siding, not shrubs, so plants near the house should be wetted down before and rinsed afterward. You can help by watering beds near the house the evening before; well-watered plants absorb less of anything that splashes on them. Point out new plantings, vegetable gardens or anything you’re especially protective of.

Pets and kids

Keep pets inside or in a part of the yard away from the work. Pressure washers are loud, hoses run across the yard, and surfaces get slippery. The same goes for kids who want to watch: from a window is best.

Water and access

Pressure washing crews generally connect to an outdoor spigot, so make sure yours works and isn’t blocked. Unlock side gates, and mention a gate code if there is one. If you won’t be home, say so when you book.

On the day

Most driveways and sidewalks take one to two hours, and a full house wash usually takes a few hours. You’ll get a time window when we quote the job. Concrete dries within an hour or two on a sunny day, though it may look a little darker until it’s fully dry.

After the cleaning

  • Give concrete a little time to dry before judging the color.
  • Rinse any plants near the house again if you like.
  • Put furniture back once surfaces are dry.
  • Think about sealing the driveway if you want to slow down the next round of staining.

Questions

Prep questions

Still unsure? Call or text (225) 363-5220.

Do I need to be home during pressure washing?

Ask when you book. If you will not be there, make sure gates are unlocked, pets are inside and an outdoor spigot is working, and mention anything the crew should avoid.

Should I move my car off the driveway?

Yes. Move vehicles off the driveway and away from the house before the crew arrives so the whole slab can be cleaned evenly and nothing gets splashed.

Will pressure washing hurt my plants?

Plants near the work area should be wetted before and rinsed after a soft wash. Watering beds the evening before also helps. Point out delicate beds or new plantings when you book.

Should I close my windows?

Yes. Close all windows and doors on the sides of the house being cleaned, and make sure they are latched tightly.

What happens if it rains?

Light rain does not usually stop concrete cleaning. Lightning and heavy storms do, and the job gets moved to another day.
