How often should you pressure wash your house in Louisiana?

A simple cleaning schedule for South Louisiana’s heat and humidity, the factors that mean you’ll need it sooner, and the signs it’s already time.

The simple schedule for South Louisiana

In much of the country the answer is once every couple of years. In South Louisiana it’s more often, because our heat and humidity grow mold, mildew and algae faster than almost anywhere. As a rule of thumb:

SurfaceHow often
House exterior (soft wash)About once a year
DrivewayOnce or twice a year
Sidewalks and walkwaysOnce or twice a year
Covered patios and pool decksOnce or twice a year
Garbage cansWhenever they smell

Those are starting points. Some homes need it more often, and the sections below explain why and how to tell.

Why Louisiana is harder on your home

Three things stack up here. First, humidity: warm, moist air is the perfect environment for the algae and mildew that streak siding and darken concrete. Second, rain and shade: frequent rain keeps surfaces damp, and anything under tree cover or on the north side of the house dries slowly and grows the green film fastest. Third, pollen and storms: heavy spring pollen coats everything, and hurricane season leaves behind grime and debris every year.

Put together, exterior surfaces in Baton Rouge simply get dirty faster than they would in a drier climate, which is why the national “every few years” advice doesn’t fit here.

Factors that mean you’ll need it more often

Shade and trees. Homes surrounded by oaks or pines, or with heavily shaded north-facing walls, grow algae fastest and often need attention twice a year.

Proximity to water or fields. Homes near bayous, ponds, rivers or open land pick up more organic growth and dust.

Highways and industry. Homes near busy roads or the river’s industrial corridor collect an extra film of airborne grime.

Light-colored surfaces. White and light siding, and light concrete, show mildew streaks much sooner than darker colors, even if the actual buildup is the same.

Roof and gutter runoff. Algae streaks that start on the roof wash down onto siding, so a roof problem becomes a whole-house problem if ignored.

Signs it’s already time

You don’t have to track dates; your house will tell you. Watch for:

  • Green or black streaks creeping across siding.
  • A driveway noticeably darker than it used to be, especially along the edges.
  • Slippery, discolored walkways and steps.
  • Dark streaks running down from the roofline.
  • A filmy, dull look to the whole exterior.
  • That moment a freshly cleaned neighbor’s house makes yours look tired.

Any one of those means it’s time. If your driveway is the problem, our guide to why driveways turn black explains what’s going on.

The best times of year to schedule

Two windows work especially well here. Late spring, after the heavy pollen has fallen, resets everything for summer. Fall, heading into the holidays, gets the house and driveway looking their best for guests and cooler-weather curb appeal. That said, we work year-round, and the honest best time is simply whenever the buildup shows.

If you’re selling, schedule right before listing photos. It’s one of the cheapest, highest-impact things you can do for a listing. See pressure washing for home sellers.

Why staying on a schedule saves money

Regular cleaning isn’t just cosmetic. Mold and algae hold moisture against your siding, roof and concrete and shorten their lifespan, and once staining sets in deep enough it becomes permanent. A yearly wash keeps buildup from ever reaching that point, so each cleaning is quick and affordable instead of a heavy restoration job.

Many of our customers simply have us out on a standing annual or twice-a-year schedule so they never have to think about it. If that sounds easier, just ask when we quote your first job.

A sample year

  1. April or May: soft wash the house and clean the driveway after the pollen settles.
  2. Summer: keep an eye on shaded walkways and covered patios, which go fastest in the heat.
  3. October or November: clean the driveway, walkways and patio again before the holidays.

Scheduling questions

How often should I pressure wash my driveway?

In the Baton Rouge climate, once or twice a year keeps concrete from developing permanent mildew staining. Shaded driveways usually need the higher end of that range.

How often should the house itself be washed?

About once a year for a soft wash of the siding is right for most Louisiana homes, though shaded, north-facing or light-colored walls may need it sooner.

When is the best time of year to pressure wash?

Late spring after the pollen falls and fall before the holidays are both ideal, but we work year-round and the real answer is whenever buildup shows.

Can you set up a recurring schedule?

Yes. Many customers have us out on a standing annual or twice-a-year schedule so they never have to remember. Just ask when we quote your job.

Is it bad to pressure wash too often?

Cleaning concrete more often than needed is not harmful when it is done correctly, but it is not necessary either. For siding, a proper soft wash once a year is usually enough because it kills the growth rather than just rinsing it.
